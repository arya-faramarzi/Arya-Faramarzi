The ancient of kermanshah

My favorite city is Kermanshah because it is a place where history does not merely rest in ruins, but breathes through the timeless grandeur of the earth, perfectly captured in this striking view of Bisotun. Here, the 17th-century Shah Abbasi Caravanserai rests like a silent sentinel of sun-warmed brick and symmetrical arches, cradled directly beneath the colossal, sun-drenched limestone face of Mount Behistun. The sheer scale of the rugged cliffside towering over the manicured pathways and peaceful stone pavilions evokes the legendary tragedies of Persian folklore.

It brings to mind the famous lines of Nezami Ganjavi, who wrote of the sculptor Farhad, whose maddening love for Princess Shirin drove him to carve his devotion directly into these very mountains: "From Farhad’s axe, a sweet echo still sighs / A cry of love that echoes through the skies." Gazing at this crisp, clear panorama where the timeless artistry of the Safavid caravanserais meets the raw, towering heights of the Zagros mountains, you can almost hear the ancient echoes of passing trade caravans and the poetic sighs of history chiseled into stone.
